Surface Talent uses AI-assisted technology to help recruiters assess job-relevant evidence consistently. This page explains what that technology does, what it does not do, and what protections you have as a candidate.

We would rather tell you this plainly than bury it. If anything here is unclear, ask us.

Why we built it this way

Surface engineering roles are frequently advertised badly. A job description often misses the process, the plant, the shift pattern and the real technical depth involved. The usual result is a pile of CVs read quickly against a vague advert, and interviews with people who were never going to fit.

Our approach is to establish what a role actually needs first, write that down as weighted requirements, and then assess candidates against those requirements using the evidence available. The software exists to make that consistent and inspectable — not to sort people faster.

What the technology does

  • Turns a role into requirements. It helps structure what we learn from an employer into a written, weighted set of requirements for that specific role.
  • Reads evidence against those requirements. It reviews the information we hold about a candidate — CV, record, and interview notes or transcripts where they exist — and identifies what supports or does not support each requirement.
  • Shows the evidence, not just a number. A fit indicator is always presented with the evidence behind it, how much of the requirement set that evidence actually covers, and how confident the assessment is.
  • Marks gaps as gaps. Where there is no evidence about a requirement, it records missing evidence.
  • Suggests what to ask. It helps produce interview questions aimed at the requirements where evidence is thin.
  • Updates when new evidence arrives. After an interview, or after a candidate corrects something, the assessment can be produced again against the same requirements.

What it does not do

  • It does not decide who gets a job. Employers make hiring decisions.
  • It does not decide who we put forward. A recruiter does, and is accountable for it.
  • It does not reject candidates automatically. There is no automatic cut-off that removes someone from consideration without a person looking.
  • It does not rank people by anything other than evidence against the stated requirements for a role.
  • It does not use protected characteristics as assessment criteria.
  • It does not score personality, culture fit, facial expression, voice, or anything inferred from how someone looks or sounds.

The safeguards

Assessment criteria come from the role

Requirements are derived from the specific role and the employer's actual needs, not from a generic template and not from a profile of who has previously been hired.

Assessment is evidence-based and inspectable

Every indicator is tied to the evidence it came from. A recruiter can see what supported a judgement and what did not. An assessment with thin evidence is visibly an assessment with thin evidence.

"No evidence" is not treated as poor performance

Missing evidence is recorded as missing, and reported separately from how well the available evidence matched. A CV that simply did not mention something is not the same as a candidate who cannot do it — and it is usually the next thing we ask about.

Concerns are recorded as reasons, not opinions

Where an assessment records something as not meeting a requirement, it is recorded against a defined reason relating to the requirement, rather than as a free-text judgement about the person.

Assessments are pinned to a version

An assessment records the version of the role requirements used at the time, so it is always clear what a candidate was assessed against, even if the role changes later.

Interview evidence can change the picture

An interview is treated as evidence, not as a formality after the software has spoken. Interview evidence can raise or lower an assessment, and often fills exactly the gaps the earlier assessment identified.

A person remains accountable

Significant decisions about a candidate remain with a recruiter, and remain reviewable. No decision about a candidate is made by software alone.

Candidates can correct the record and ask us to look again

You can tell us what your CV missed, and we will correct it. Where an assessment relied on that information, it can be produced again and a recruiter will look at the result. See our candidate privacy notice for how to ask.

We do not charge candidates

You will never pay us a fee to find work. UK law prohibits it and we would not do it anyway. See our modern slavery and ethical recruitment policy.

Your CV goes nowhere without your permission

We ask you before sending your details to an employer, every time. We do not circulate CVs speculatively.

What we do not claim

We do not claim our process is free of bias, and we would treat any recruitment technology that made that claim with suspicion. Any system built by people, trained on information produced by people, and used by people can carry bias.

What we can tell you is what the process is designed to do, what it shows the recruiter, where a person is accountable, and how you can question it. We would rather be judged on those than on a claim we cannot support.

We also do not hold a certification for any of this. We are not certified, accredited or audited against an AI standard, and we do not describe ourselves as such. Where that changes, this page will say so.

Where AI is not involved

  • Assessment processing runs only when a recruiter explicitly asks for a candidate to be assessed for a role. It does not run automatically in the background as records arrive.
  • We do not use candidate information to train publicly available AI models.
  • This website does not profile you. It carries no analytics, no session recording and no advertising technology — see our cookie policy.
